\section{Introduction}

Network traffic analysis plays a fundamental role in modern cybersecurity. By analyzing network traffic, it is possible to detect and proactively prevent malicious activities \cite{wang:2025, miguel:2026}. It is possible to identify abnormal behaviors in the network by monitoring communication patterns and data exchanges. Malicious behaviors that may indicate cyberattacks, intrusions, or policy violations [ref]. As networks continue to grow in size and complexity, automated and intelligent approaches for traffic analysis have become necessary [ref].

One approach to network traffic analysis is the use of network flows. Network flows summarize communication sessions between hosts by aggregating packets that share common characteristics, such as source and destination addresses, ports, and protocols \cite{miguel:2026}. This abstraction reduces data volume while preserving relevant behavioral information, making flow-based analysis suitable for scalable monitoring and threat detection in high-speed networks \cite{miguel:2026}.

To analyze network flows efficiently, several techniques based on artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning have been proposed \cite{folino:2021, alghushairy:2024, talukder:2024}. These techniques can be broadly categorized into supervised and unsupervised approaches. Supervised methods rely on labeled data and often require detailed contextual information, including prior knowledge of attack characteristics \cite{sharma:2025, miguel:2026}. While effective in many scenarios, this dependency makes them less suitable for detecting zero-day attacks, where the attack patterns are unknown or significantly different from previously observed threats [ref zero-days] \cite{miguel:2026}.

In this context, weightless neural networks, such as the WiSARD model, emerge as a promising alternative [ref]. WiSARD does not rely on traditional weight-based learning and can operate with reduced dependence on explicit attack signatures or contextual data [ref]. However, applying weightless neural networks to flow-based traffic analysis introduces significant challenges[ref]. One of these challenges concerns the encoding of flow data into binary representations suitable for this type of model [ref termometros, etc.].

To address these challenges, this work proposes a novel method for encoding network flow data tailored to weightless neural networks. The proposed encoding strategy is evaluated using real-world traffic data from Rede Rio. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach is capable of effectively detecting network threats, highlighting its potential as a viable solution for flow-based intrusion detection, especially in scenarios involving unknown or emerging attacks.
